# Snapshot: PCV (pneumococcal conjugate) — CDC schedule notes, catch-up
# intervals, and the two currently-recommended products' inserts

Fetched live 2026-09-25 via the `verify-clinical-source` skill.

## CDC Child Immunization Schedule Notes, 2025 — Pneumococcal
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/child-adolescent-notes.html
Page last reviewed: July 2, 2025

> 4-dose series at 2, 4, 6, 12–15 months

> Healthy children ages 2–4 years with any incomplete* PCV series: 1 dose PCV

> For children without risk conditions, PCV20 is not indicated if they have
> received 4 doses of PCV13 or PCV15 or another age appropriate complete PCV
> series.

> minimum age: 6 weeks [PCV15], [PCV20]; 2 years [PPSV23]

This app plans a healthy child with no prior vaccines, so the catch-up/
incomplete-series and brand-switching notes above don't change the on-time
plan (4 doses, one currently-recommended product family, 2/4/6/12-15
months) — noted for the rulebook, not modeled as a schedule branch, per
CLAUDE.md's v1 scope (no catch-up logic).

## CDC child/adolescent catch-up table — Pneumococcal minimum intervals
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/schedules/wcms-inc/child-schedule-table-catchup_TP4.html

> Minimum age for dose 1: 6 weeks

> Dose 1 to dose 2: 4 weeks if first dose administered before the 1st
> birthday; 8 weeks (as final dose for healthy children) if first dose was
> administered at the 1st birthday or after

> Dose 2 to dose 3: 4 weeks if current age is younger than 12 months and
> previous dose given at <7 months old; 8 weeks (as final dose for healthy
> children) if previous dose given between 7-11 months (wait until at least
> 12 months old)

> Dose 3 to dose 4: 8 weeks (as final dose). This dose is only necessary for
> children age 12 through 59 months regardless of risk ... who received 3
> doses before age 12 months.

These are the same shape of minimum-interval floors as HepB/Hib (dose 4's
"only necessary if ..." sentence describes exactly the on-time 2/4/6/12-15
path this app plans, since an on-time child always takes all 3 primary
doses before 12 months). Modeled as `minAgeDays` / `minIntervalFromPrevDays`
on each dose in `series.js`, matching the existing HepB/Hib pattern — no
`variants[]` needed here since both current products cover doses 1-4
identically (unlike Hib/RV/MenB, there is no brand choice that changes the
series length).

## Prevnar 20 package insert (DailyMed, label revision 4/2023)
URL: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=d4e2cf51-e6a8-4103-bb1d-6120c6474ff8&type=display
Licensed age range in children: 6 weeks through 17 years of age.

> Administer Prevnar 20 as a 4-dose series at 2, 4, 6, and 12 through 15
> months of age (and at least 2 months after the third dose). The first
> dose may be given as early as 6 weeks of age.

## Vaxneuvance package insert (DailyMed, label revision 6/2026)
URL: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=1158fa93-ef41-4a29-8252-9251f94c53c8&type=display

> VAXNEUVANCE is a vaccine indicated for active immunization ... in
> individuals 6 weeks of age and older.

> Administer VAXNEUVANCE as a 4-dose series at 2, 4, 6 and 12 through 15
> months of age.

The first dose may be administered as early as 6 weeks of age, and the
fourth dose must be given at least 2 months after the third dose (same
DailyMed page, dosage section).

Both current inserts agree exactly with CDC/ACIP — no insert-narrower
conflict for either product.

## Conclusion

Modeled as a plain 4-dose series (like HepB), no `variants[]`. Two current
products (Prevnar 20, Vaxneuvance), one retired product (Prevnar 13, dose
licence carried over unchanged from before — its own retirement date is
still unverified, see above; its PCV7 predecessor's history is already
recorded in docs/decisions.md and does not need re-verifying here).
