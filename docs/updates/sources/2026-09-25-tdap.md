# Snapshot: Tdap — CDC schedule notes, catch-up minimum age, and both
# single-antigen products' inserts

Fetched live 2026-09-25 via the `verify-clinical-source` skill.

## CDC Child Immunization Schedule Notes, 2025 — Tdap
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/child-adolescent-notes.html
Page last reviewed: July 2, 2025

> Age 11–12 years: 1 dose Tdap (adolescent booster)

> Age 10 years who receive Tdap do not need the adolescent Tdap booster
> dose at age 11–12 years.

The second quote is a catch-up allowance (an early Tdap at 10 counts) —
out of scope for this app's v1 (CLAUDE.md: "no catch-up logic"), since a
healthy on-time child simply takes the one routine dose at 11-12 years.
Not modeled.

## CDC child/adolescent catch-up table — Tdap minimum age
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/schedules/wcms-inc/child-schedule-table-catchup_TP4.html

> Minimum age for dose 1: 7 years (catch-up table, ages 7 through 18)

This is the catch-up-table floor (used for children who missed DTaP and
are getting Tdap as a substitute primary/catch-up dose), not the routine
adolescent-booster age. This app plans a healthy on-time child who already
completed the DTaP series, so the relevant minimum age is the routine one
(11 years) from the schedule notes above, not this catch-up floor.

## Adacel package insert (DailyMed, label revision 8/2026)
URL: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a41b7601-34f2-4a88-a406-f53011fb7de1&type=display

> Adacel is approved for use in persons 10 through 64 years of age.

> Administer each dose (0.5 mL) intramuscularly.

## Boostrix package insert (DailyMed, label revision 3/2025)
URL: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=c3c03458-942c-4565-8a9c-1901bb0d2db0&type=display

> Active booster immunization against tetanus, diphtheria, and pertussis
> in individuals aged 10 years and older.

Both inserts allow the single dose from age 10 — a year *wider* than
CDC/ACIP's routine 11-year age, not narrower, so no gap. CDC/ACIP's 11-12
year routine age governs the on-time plan; both products are licensed
widely enough to cover it either way.

## Conclusion

Modeled as a single-dose series at the 11- or 12-year visit. No
`variants[]` — Adacel and Boostrix cover the one dose identically.
