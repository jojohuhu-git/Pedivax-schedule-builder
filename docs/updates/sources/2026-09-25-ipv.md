# Snapshot: IPV (inactivated poliovirus) — CDC schedule notes, catch-up
# intervals, and the single-antigen product's insert

Fetched live 2026-09-25 via the `verify-clinical-source` skill.

## CDC Child Immunization Schedule Notes, 2025 — IPV
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/child-adolescent-notes.html
Page last reviewed: July 2, 2025

> 4-dose series at ages 2, 4, 6–18 months, 4–6 years; administer the final
> dose on or after age 4 years and at least 6 months after the previous
> dose.

> 4 or more doses of IPV can be administered before age 4 years when a
> combination vaccine containing IPV is used. However, a dose is still
> recommended on or after age 4 years and at least 6 months after the
> previous dose.

The second quote is why Pentacel (DTaP-IPV-Hib, doses 1-4) doesn't finish
the IPV series on its own even though it reaches dose 4 by 15-18 months —
an on-time child taking Pentacel through dose 4 still needs a 5th IPV dose
at 4-6 years (this is exactly the Quadracel "fourth or fifth dose" licence
seen in decisions.md). Relevant when the combo products are added; not
modeled as a schedule branch in this plain series entry.

## CDC child/adolescent catch-up table — IPV minimum intervals
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/schedules/wcms-inc/child-schedule-table-catchup_TP4.html

> Minimum age for dose 1: 6 weeks

> Dose 1 to dose 2: 4 weeks

> Dose 2 to dose 3: 4 weeks if current age is <4 years; 6 months (as final
> dose) if current age is 4 years or older

> Dose 3 to dose 4: 6 months (minimum age 4 years for final dose)

> A fourth dose is not necessary if the third dose was administered at age
> 4 years or older and at least 6 months after the previous dose.

That exception can't occur in this app's on-time plan (dose 3 always lands
at 6-18 months, well before age 4), so — same treatment as DTaP/PCV's
equivalent exceptions — it's not modeled as a schedule branch. The absolute
floor that DOES bind on-time is real, though: dose 4 needs both age ≥4
years AND ≥6 months since dose 3, so it's placed at the 4-year visit, not
earlier, even though the visit calendar has y3 before it.

## IPOL package insert (DailyMed, label revision 6/2026)
URL: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=34a647f5-8728-451b-b918-94c8acd15974&type=display
Licensed age range: infants (as young as 6 weeks of age), children, and
adults.

> The primary series of IPOL vaccine consists of three 0.5 mL doses
> administered intramuscularly or subcutaneously, preferably eight or more
> weeks apart and usually at ages 2, 4, and 6 to 18 months.

> For this series, a booster dose of IPOL vaccine is administered at 4 to 6
> years of age.

> The first immunization may be administered as early as six weeks of age.

IPOL is the only single-antigen IPV product on the mockup's formulary list
— agrees with CDC/ACIP exactly, no gap.

## Kinrix / Quadracel / Pediarix / Pentacel / Vaxelis — still NOT added

IPV is now verified, which was the last antigen Pediarix needed (DTaP +
HepB + IPV, all three done). Pentacel and Vaxelis also only needed DTaP +
IPV (Hib already done). Kinrix and Quadracel likewise. All five combo
products can be added next, reusing the lineage quotes already verified in
docs/decisions.md 2026-09-24 (Kinrix/Quadracel) — no need to re-fetch those.

## Conclusion

IPV modeled as a plain 4-dose series (like HepB/PCV/DTaP), no `variants[]`.
