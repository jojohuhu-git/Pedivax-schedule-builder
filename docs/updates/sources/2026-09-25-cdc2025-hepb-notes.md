## Quoted content (Hepatitis B, routine vaccination, mother HBsAg-negative)

> 3-dose series at ages 0, 1–2, and 6–18 months

> Birth weight ≥2,000 grams: 1 dose within 24 hours of birth if medically stable

> Birth weight <2,000 grams: 1 dose at chronological age 1 month or hospital discharge

> use monovalent HepB vaccine for doses administered before age 6 weeks

> Final (3rd or 4th) dose: age 6–18 months (minimum age 24 weeks)

> Administration of 4 doses is permitted when a combination vaccine containing HepB
> is used after the birth dose

## Re-checked 2026-09-26, same URL and same July 2, 2025 edition — the 4-dose substitution rule

Prompted by a real bug the `cover.test.js` exhaustive check caught: Pediarix and
Vaxelis had been modeled as covering HepB doses 1-3 (copied from their DTaP/IPV
pattern), which is impossible — neither is licensed before 6 weeks, and HepB
dose 1 is specifically the birth dose. Fetched live again to find exactly how
the 4-dose numbering works:

> Minimum intervals (see Table 2): when 4 doses are administered, substitute
> "dose 4" for "dose 3" in these calculations

This is the complete rule CDC states for the 4-dose case — it does not give a
separate, distinct minimum interval for "dose 2 to dose 3" in the 4-dose
numbering. Read plainly, it means: whichever administration ends up being the
last one keeps the old "dose 3" gating (≥8 weeks after the previous dose, ≥16
weeks after dose 1, ≥24 weeks of age — matches the "Final (3rd or 4th) dose"
line above, quoted 2026-09-25), and every dose before that is an ordinary
primary-series step. No CDC page found in this session states a different
number for the earlier step, so `src/data/series.js`'s 4-dose ("combo") variant
uses the same 4-week (28-day) floor as dose 1→dose 2 for dose 2→dose 3 — the
same default spacing every other primary series in this app uses for its
non-final doses (DTaP, IPV, PCV, Hib) — rather than inventing an unstated
number. This is a reasoned application of the quoted substitution rule, not a
separately quoted sentence; flagged here so a future session re-reads it if a
more specific source ever turns up.
