# Snapshot: Hepatitis A — CDC schedule notes, catch-up intervals, and both
# single-antigen products' inserts

Fetched live 2026-09-25 via the `verify-clinical-source` skill.

## CDC Child Immunization Schedule Notes, 2025 — Hepatitis A
URL: https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/child-adolescent-notes.html
Page last reviewed: July 2, 2025

> 2-dose series (minimum interval: 6 months) at age 12–23 months

Both doses land in the 12-23-month window, with a 6-month floor between
them — unlike every other 2-dose series in this app so far (MMR/Varicella
put the second dose years later at 4-6 years), so the two on-time visits
that satisfy the 6-month floor depend on which visit dose 1 lands at: dose
1 at 12 months allows dose 2 at 18 months exactly; dose 1 at 15 months
needs dose 2 no earlier than 21 months, so the next standard visit that
works is 24 months. Modeled with both m18 and m24 as valid dose-2 visits
and the real interval doing the gating, rather than hardcoding one visit
the way the frozen mockup did (`{ok:['m18']}` alone) — that would silently
violate the 6-month floor for a child whose dose 1 ran at 15 months.

## Havrix package insert (DailyMed, label revision 11/2013 — unusually old,
## flagged to re-check, same treatment as Engerix-B)
URL: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=135ff1b4-c21f-4df1-97be-9475680e9e44&type=display
Licensed age range: 12 months through 18 years.

> A single 0.5-mL dose and a 0.5-mL booster dose administered between 6 to
> 12 months later.

The insert's own dose-2 ceiling (12 months after dose 1) is narrower than
CDC's floor-only rule allows for, but CDC/ACIP sets no ceiling here at
all — only a 6-month minimum — so there's nothing for the insert to
narrow. Not modeled as a gap.

## Vaqta package insert (DailyMed, label revision 9/2025)
URL: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=89191ae9-7f2b-4206-8397-bf7fce3436ac&type=display
Licensed age range: 12 months of age and older.

> Children/Adolescents: vaccination consists of a 0.5-mL primary dose
> administered intramuscularly, and a 0.5-mL booster dose administered
> intramuscularly 6 to 18 months later.

Both inserts agree with CDC/ACIP's 6-month minimum interval — no
insert-narrower conflict for either product.

## Conclusion

Modeled as a 2-dose series with a real minimum-interval floor that
actually changes which on-time visit is valid for dose 2 (m18 vs m24),
unlike every prior series where the floor was moot. No `variants[]` —
Havrix and Vaqta cover doses 1-2 identically.
